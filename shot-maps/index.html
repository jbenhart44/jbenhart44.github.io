How to read a map

The half court is cut into hexagons 3 feet across, with one centered on the rim. For each player and hexagon: size shows how many shots he took there; color is his field-goal percentage there minus the league's from the same hexagon that season, with his own shots counted in the league. Orange is better than the league, blue is worse, and the scale stops at 15 percentage points either way. A hexagon where he took fewer than 10 shots is drawn as a gray outline and not colored, because a handful of shots says more about luck than skill.

Three other settings sit behind buttons on each season page: a floor of 5 shots, hexagons 5 feet across, and color pooled from each hexagon and the six around it. They cover more of the court and trade away some precision. They are there to compare, not a different result.

Where the numbers come from

Shot locations are NBA.com's shot logs as collected in the DomSamangy/NBA_Shots_04_25 repository. Points per game, games played, and the cross-checks come from Basketball-Reference. The games minimum is quoted from NBA.com's statistical minimums and Wikipedia's list of scoring leaders. Each season page lists its files with download dates and sha256 fingerprints, and every check it passed.
