Motivation

One issue that hoop enthusiasts run into when comparing players from different eras is the relativity of statistics, and this has bothered me for some time.

Here, I propose an admittedly imperfect solution: instead of comparing player stats from different eras, why don't we compare the statistical performance of players to the league-average of that year? Extending that, we can compare the relative performance of players from one year to the relative performance of another player from another era.

Example: Ray Allen and Stephen Curry

First, we compare the raw shooting of Stephen Curry to Ray Allen. Ray Allen came to the NBA in 1996 and Stephen Curry came to the NBA in 2009 -- it would make sense to me that Allen will (1) shoot less threes and (2) make less threes because the average team shot 1.3 less three-pointers per game in '96 as compared to '09.

Beyond that, intra-season factors can have a profound impact on the statistics for players within a season (see this article about the statistical changes following a directive to allow more physical play during the 2023-2024 season.)

Instead of comparing the raw shooting stats of Stephen Curry to the raw shooting stats of Ray Allen, these diagrams (1) assess Ray Allen's performance to other NBA players during the specific years that he played, (2) assess Stephen Curry's relative performance, and then (3) differentiate the performances of the two individuals on one interpretable heat map.

    Details
    • Each hexagon is a patch of the half court 5 feet across.
    • Size: how often he shot from that patch. It is full size at 50 attempts or more.
    • Color: his field-goal percentage there and in the six hexagons around it, minus the league's from the same seven, that season. Red is better than the league, blue is worse, and the scale stops at 15 points.
    • A gray outline: fewer than 10 shots across the seven, too few to color.
    • Two players on one map: orange where the first shot better against his own league, blue where the second did. Only the spots where both took 10 or more shots are drawn.
    • Hover a hexagon, or tap it on a phone, for its numbers.
