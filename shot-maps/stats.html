On the maps and the cards

Hexagon size

How many shots he took from that patch of the half court. Each hexagon is 3 feet across. It is drawn full size from 50 attempts up, and smaller below that.

Hexagon color

His field-goal percentage from that hexagon minus the whole league's from the same hexagon that season, with his own shots counted in the league's. Orange is better than the league and blue is worse. The scale stops at 15 percentage points either way.

The 10-shot floor

A hexagon where he took fewer than 10 shots is drawn as a gray outline and not colored, because a handful of shots says more about luck than skill.

Pooled view

Colors each hexagon from it and the six around it together, still needing 10 shots, so more of the court gets a color. In the pooled view a hexagon's color comes from it and the six around it, but its size is still his own attempts in that one hexagon, so a small hexagon can carry a strong color its neighbours earned. Hover or tap it to see how many shots the color rests on.

Points per game

From Basketball-Reference's per-game table. Each season page shows the 20 highest among players who met that season's scoring-title rule.

Games

Games he played that season, from Basketball-Reference's per-game table.

Half-court attempts

His field-goal attempts from the half court in NBA.com's shot data. Shots from the backcourt are left out, and free throws are not field-goal attempts.

Points per shot

Points from his made half-court field goals, 2 or 3 for each, divided by his half-court attempts. Free throws are not counted.

Shot types

NBA.com's label for each attempt (jump shot, driving layup, dunk and so on), with his field-goal percentage on each and the league's on the same type. A type with fewer than 10 attempts is listed but not compared. The labels changed over the years, so compare shot types within a season, not across seasons.

Advanced stats on every card

PER, Win Shares, BPM and VORP are Basketball-Reference's own models of a player's all-around value. The shot maps do not measure them, and this site has no second source to check them against. The one advanced figure this site recomputes itself is eFG%, from the shot data. Every quotation below is checked, word for word, against a saved copy of the page it comes from before this page is built.

eFG% Effective Field Goal Percentage

This statistic adjusts for the fact that a 3-point field goal is worth one more point than a 2-point field goal.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

TS% True Shooting Percentage

A measure of shooting efficiency that takes into account 2-point field goals, 3-point field goals, and free throws.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

PER Player Efficiency Rating

A measure of per-minute production standardized such that the league average is 15.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

A per-minute rating that adds up a player's positive accomplishments and subtracts the negative ones, scaled so that the league average is 15 in every season.

In Basketball-Reference's words:

The Player Efficiency Rating (PER) is a per-minute rating developed by ESPN.com columnist John Hollinger.Calculating PER, Basketball-Reference
In John's words, "The PER sums up all a player's positive accomplishments, subtracts the negative accomplishments, and returns a per-minute rating of a player's performance."Calculating PER, Basketball-Reference
The step above sets the league average to 15 for all seasons.Calculating PER, Basketball-Reference
USG% Usage Percentage

An estimate of the percentage of team plays used by a player while they were on the floor.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

WS Win Shares

An estimate of the number of wins contributed by a player.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

Credit for a team's success, divided among its players and counted in wins. One Win Share is one win, a team's players add up to roughly its win total, and it splits into offense (OWS) and defense (DWS). WS/48 is the same per 48 minutes.

In Basketball-Reference's words:

Win Shares is a player statistic which attempts to divvy up credit for team success to the individuals on the team.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
the sum of player win shares on a given team will be roughly equal to that team's win total for the seasonNBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
In my system, one win is equivalent to one Win Share.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
In my system, a player can have negative Win Shares.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
a player with negative Win Shares was so poor that he essentially took away wins that his teammates had generated.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
Offensive Win Shares are credited to players based on Dean Oliver's points produced and offensive possessions.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
Crediting Defensive Win Shares to players is based on Dean Oliver's Defensive Rating.NBA Win Shares, Basketball-Reference
WS/48 Win Shares Per 48 Minutes

An estimate of the number of wins contributed by a player per 48 minutes (league average is approximately .100)

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

Part of WS.

BPM Box Plus/Minus

A box score estimate of the points per 100 possessions a player contributed above a league-average player, translated to an average team.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

Points per 100 possessions a player adds above a league-average player, estimated from the box score. 0 is average, and it ignores how much he played.

In Basketball-Reference's words:

Box Plus/Minus, Version 2.0 (BPM) is a basketball box score-based metric that estimates a basketball player's contribution to the team when that player is on the court.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
BPM uses a player's box score information, position, and the team's overall performance to estimate the player's contribution in points above league average per 100 possessions played.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
BPM does not take into account playing time -- it is purely a rate stat!Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
League average is defined as 0.0, meaning 0 points above average or below average.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
Box Plus/Minus is good at measuring offense and solid overall, but the defensive numbers in particular should not be considered definitive.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference

Basketball-Reference's scale for BPM:

  • +10.0 is an all-time season (think peak Jordan or LeBron)
  • +8.0 is an MVP season (think peak Dirk or peak Shaq)
  • +6.0 is an all-NBA season
  • +4.0 is in all-star consideration
  • +2.0 is a good starter
  • +0.0 is a decent starter or solid 6th man
  • -2.0 is a bench player (this is also defined as "replacement level")
VORP Value over Replacement Player

A box score estimate of the points per 100 TEAM possessions that a player contributed above a replacement-level (-2.0) player, translated to an average team and prorated to an 82-game season. Multiply by 2.70 to convert to wins over replacement.

Basketball-Reference's column-header definition.

BPM turned into an estimate of a player's overall contribution: how much he added over a replacement-level player (a BPM of -2.0), scaled by how much he played.

In Basketball-Reference's words:

Value over Replacement Player (VORP) converts the BPM rate into an estimate of each player's overall contribution to the team, measured vs. what a theoretical "replacement player" would provide, where the "replacement player" is defined as a player on minimum salary or not a normal member of a team's rotation.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
The conclusion was to establish -2.0 as replacement level for the NBA, measured in terms of points above or below average per 100 possessions.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
So, to calculate VORP, the formula is simply: [BPM - (-2.0)] * (% of possessions played) * (team games/82).Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
To convert VORP to an estimate of wins over replacement, simply multiply by 2.7.Box Plus/Minus 2.0 (BPM), Basketball-Reference
