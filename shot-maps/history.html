Where the top scorers shoot from

  • 2025-26: the top 20's mid-range share was 8.3 points higher than everyone else's (17.4% against 9.1%), and their three-point share 7.5 points lower (34.9% against 42.4%).
  • 2020-21: the top 20's mid-range share was 6.2 points higher than everyone else's (18.3% against 12.1%), and their three-point share 7.4 points lower (32.5% against 40.0%).
  • 2015-16: the top 20's mid-range share was 2.9 points higher than everyone else's (27.2% against 24.3%), and their three-point share 1.2 points higher (29.4% against 28.1%).
  • 2010-11: the top 20's mid-range share was 3.3 points higher than everyone else's (34.0% against 30.6%), and their three-point share 8.0 points lower (15.1% against 23.1%).
  • 2005-06: the top 20's mid-range share was 3.8 points higher than everyone else's (37.7% against 33.9%), and their three-point share 0.2 points lower (19.9% against 20.1%).
  • 2003-04: the top 20's mid-range share was 3.0 points higher than everyone else's (38.3% against 35.3%), and their three-point share 0.7 points lower (18.0% against 18.7%).
  • In all 23 seasons the top 20 took a larger share of their half-court shots from mid-range than everyone else, and in 22 of 23 a smaller share of threes (the exception: 2015-16).
