Scoring in the NBA: An Overview

The goal of this page is to give a high-level view of league-wide trends and their impact on scoring (and therefore the game in general.) Considering these changes should help us as fans understand and contextualize player performances from era to era.

The Best Scorers Shoot More Midrange Shots than Everybody Else

In each of the seasons considered, the top scorers took a greater share of their shots from midrange than the average NBA player. This doesn't mean that they took more midrange shots than threes in total (in 2025-26, 17.4% of their shots were midrange and 34.9% were threes), but it shows that the midrange game is dying faster for those who aren't the "go-to" guy on their team.

Where the top scorers shoot from

Both groups moved out to the three-point line, but the top 20 moved less. Their mid-range share fell 17.7 points (35.2% in 1996-97 to 17.4% in 2025-26); the non-top 20's fell 19.8 points (28.9% to 9.1%).

Against his own league

Each dot is one season’s scoring leader. Across: how much of his team’s shooting he did. Up: how much better than his league he shot (eFG%, which counts a made three as one and a half makes). Above the gray line, he shot better than his league; below it, worse.

  • The most efficient scoring title against its own league: Stephen Curry in 2015-16, whose eFG% was 12.8 points above the league's.
  • The most volume: Allen Iverson in 2001-02, who took 35.1% of his team's shots.
  • 7 of 30 scoring leaders shot below their league: Michael Jordan 1997-98 (-0.5); Allen Iverson 1998-99 (-2.7); Allen Iverson 2000-01 (-2.6); Allen Iverson 2001-02 (-5.6); Allen Iverson 2004-05 (-3.0); Russell Westbrook 2014-15 (-4.1); Russell Westbrook 2016-17 (-3.9).

Shot Efficiency

In all 30 seasons the top 20 scored more points per shot than the non-top 20. The gap was widest in 1997-98: 0.046 points a shot. These count field goals only: the shot data has no free throws.

Points

The average top-20 scorer went from 22.7 points per game in 1996-97 to 26.2 in 2025-26 (the highest was 27.4, in 2022-23). The 20th-best went from 20.1 to 21.7. The best single season: James Harden, 36.1 in 2018-19.

So what changed?

  • Scoring is up: the average top-20 scorer scores 3.5 more points per game than in 1996-97.
  • The league moved to the three-point line faster than its best scorers did: in 2025-26 the non-top 20 took 42.4% of their shots as threes, the top 20 34.9%.
  • The best scorers stay more efficient than the rest: more points per shot in all 30 seasons.
  • A scoring title is volume, not always efficiency: 7 of 30 leaders shot below their league, while Stephen Curry in 2015-16 shot 12.8 points above it.

  • Paint: the rest of the painted lane.
