The league's leading scorers take more of their shots from mid-range than the league does. In all 22 seasons the top 20 took a larger share of their half-court shots from mid-range than the league as a whole did, and in 21 of 22 they took a smaller share of threes (the exception: 2015-16). Both groups moved the same way. From 2003-04 to 2024-25 the top 20's share of threes rose from 18.0% to 38.0%, and the league's rose from 18.6% to 42.0%. Their mid-range share fell from 38.3% to 15.0%, and the league's fell from 35.7% to 9.8%.

Points

The top 20 averaged 21.8 points a game in 2003-04 and 25.8 in 2024-25; the highest was 27.4, in 2022-23. The 20th-best scorer averaged 18.7 in 2003-04 and 22.8 in 2024-25, and the season's leader 28.0 and 32.7. The highest leading average was 36.1, James Harden in 2018-19.

How well

In all 22 seasons the top 20 scored more points per half-court shot than the league. Theirs rose from 0.961 in 2003-04 to 1.109 in 2024-25, and the league's rose from 0.944 to 1.089. These count field goals only: the shot data has no free throws.

How much

The top 20 took 17.7 field-goal attempts a game in 2003-04 and 18.7 in 2024-25, counted from the shot data (which can fall short of the official count by up to 0.5%).

Every number on this page

Zone columns are the top 20's share of their half-court shots. Everything is counted from the same shot data as the maps, by tools/history.py, which refuses a season whose zone counts do not add up, whose top-20 attempts are not the season pages' count, or whose league three-point share is more than half a point from Basketball-Reference's. This page describes what changed; it does not say why.
