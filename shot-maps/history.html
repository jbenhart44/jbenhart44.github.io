Scoring in the NBA: An Overview

The goal of this page is to give a high-level view of league-wide trends and their impact on scoring (and therefore the game in general.) Considering these changes should help us as fans understand and contextualize player performances from era to era.

Share of Attempts, season by season

The pies from the top of each season’s page, for every season from 1996-97 to 2025-26, one season per second.

Shot Zones

  1. Three - shot beyond the 3pt line
  2. Midrange - shot inside the 3pt line taken outside of the paint
  3. Paint - shot inside of the paint taken outside of the restricted area
  4. At the Rim - shot taken inside of the restricted area
  • Threes went from 21.1% of the league's shots in 1996-97 to 41.5% in 2025-26, and midrange fell from 29.8% to 10.1%.
  • Shots at the rim fell from 37.2% to 28.4% over the same seasons.

The Top 20 Take Fewer Threes and Still Shoot Better

Top 20Non-top 20

  • In 28 of 30 seasons the top 20 took a smaller share of their shots as threes than the non-top 20 (not in 2001-02 and 2015-16), and their eFG% was higher in every one of them. In 2025-26, 34.9% of their shots were threes against 42.4%, and their eFG% was 55.0% against 54.5%.
  • Their eFG% was higher in all 30 seasons: most in 1997-98 (49.9% against 47.5%), least in 2019-20 (53.1% against 53.0%).
  • By era, the top 20's lead in three-point percentage was 0.2, 0.7, 1.0 and 0.9 points, against 1.9 to 4.8 points at the rim, in the paint and from midrange. The non-top 20 shot threes better in 12 of 30 seasons, by at most 2.3 points.

It Isn't Where They Shoot From

Top 20Non-top 20Non-top 20, if they took the top 20's mix of shots

  • It is not where they shoot from: if the non-top 20 took the top 20's mix of shots and made each zone at their own percentage, they would still score fewer points per shot in all 30 seasons.
  • At the rim, in the paint and from midrange, the non-top 20's field-goal percentage was higher than the top 20's in none of the 30 seasons.
  • From 2020-21 to 2025-26 they made 48.0% of their shots in the paint outside the restricted area against 43.2%, a lead of 4.8 points. At the rim their lead was 2.3 points, and from midrange 3.6.

The Best Scorers Shoot More Midrange Shots than Everybody Else

Top 20Non-top 20

  • In all 30 seasons the top 20 took a larger share of their shots from midrange than the non-top 20. In 2025-26: 17.4% against 9.1%.
  • From the Midrange Era to the Threes and Paint era, the non-top 20 went from 55,266 to 19,784 midrange shots a season, and their midrange percentage rose from 38.8% to 40.9%. The top 20 went from 10,375 to 4,497, and from 40.7% to 44.5%.

A Scoring Title Is Volume, Not Always Efficiency

Each dot is one season’s scoring leader. Across: how much of his team’s shooting he did. Up: how much better than his league he shot. Above the gray line, better; below it, worse.

  • The most efficient scoring title against its own league: Stephen Curry in 2015-16, whose eFG% was 12.8 points above the league's.
  • The most volume: Allen Iverson in 2001-02, who took 35.1% of his team's shots.
  • 7 of 30 scoring leaders shot below their league: Michael Jordan 1997-98 (-0.5); Allen Iverson 1998-99 (-2.7); Allen Iverson 2000-01 (-2.6); Allen Iverson 2001-02 (-5.6); Allen Iverson 2004-05 (-3.0); Russell Westbrook 2014-15 (-4.1); Russell Westbrook 2016-17 (-3.9).
