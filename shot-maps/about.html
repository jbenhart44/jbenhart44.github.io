How to read a map

The half court is cut into hexagons 5 feet across, with one centered on the rim. Size is about attempts and color is about efficiency. For each player and hexagon: size shows how many shots he took in it; color is his field-goal percentage in it and the six hexagons around it, minus the league's from the same seven that season, with his own shots counted in the league. Orange is better than the league, blue is worse, and the scale stops at 15 percentage points either way, deepening fastest near zero. A hexagon where he took fewer than 10 shots across the seven is drawn as a gray outline and not colored, because a handful of shots says more about luck than skill. Hover a hexagon for its numbers; on a phone, tap it and they appear under the map.

Comparing two scorers on the home page draws one map for the pair, from the same season. A hexagon's color is the first scorer's field-goal percentage in it and the six around it, minus the second's: orange where the first shot better, blue where the second did, on the same 15-point scale. It is colored only where both took at least 10 shots across the seven and at least one in the hexagon itself. Its size is the smaller of their two attempt counts in the hexagon, so a big hexagon is one both of them shot from often.

The orange-to-blue scale was checked for the three common kinds of color blindness (protanomaly, deuteranomaly, tritanomaly) with Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes' published simulation: the two ends stay clearly apart for all three.

Shot types

Each card lists the shot types NBA.com recorded for the player (jump shot, driving layup, dunk and so on), with his field-goal percentage on each and the league's on the same type. A type with fewer than 10 attempts is listed but not compared. The made-dunk count is checked against Basketball-Reference's for every player.

NBA.com's labels are not the same in every season. It used fewer, broader labels early on, so "Jump Shot" covers far more of the league's attempts in the early seasons than in recent ones. Compare shot types within a season, not across seasons:

Advanced stats

Each player card also carries his season's advanced stats from Basketball-Reference. They come in three kinds, and the pages say which is which:

  • eFG% is checked two ways: recomputed from the shot data and compared with Basketball-Reference's figure.
  • TS% and the rate stats are Basketball-Reference's arithmetic on box-score totals. The shot data has no free throws, so this site cannot recompute them.
  • PER, Win Shares, BPM and VORP are Basketball-Reference's own models of all-around value. They are shown as Basketball-Reference publishes them, with the page and date they were taken from; nothing here can check them independently, and the maps do not measure them.

Every stat's meaning is on What the numbers mean.

Where the numbers come from

Shot locations and shot types are NBA.com's shot logs as collected in the DomSamangy/NBA_Shots_04_25 repository. Points per game, games played, the advanced stats, dunk counts and the cross-checks come from Basketball-Reference, a Sports Reference LLC site. The qualifying rule is quoted from NBA.com's statistical minimums, Basketball-Reference's rate statistic requirements (for the seasons before 2013-14, when a player also qualified by points) and Wikipedia's list of scoring leaders. Each season page lists its files with download dates and sha256 fingerprints, and every check it passed.
